# L02f2: bounded repair and retest

## Terms

- **Candidate** — `e1000_l02`, the driver written from spec revision 4 alone (L02e).
- **Reference** — Linux v6.12's `e1000` driver, which the candidate is compared with.
- **Round** — one repair by the implementer, then an audit of its command log, a build, and
  an isolated rerun of all ten scenarios.
- **Implementer** — the agent that edits the candidate; here Codex (codex-cli 0.157.0, model
  `gpt-6-astra`), launched as a separate program on the test host.
- **Sandbox / audit** — bubblewrap (a Linux tool that runs a program in a private view of the
  file system) shows the implementer only its workspace; strace (a Linux tool that logs a
  program's system calls) records every file it opened, and `sandbox_audit.py` checks that log.
  A **canary** is a file the implementer is asked to read in a pilot, to prove the log sees it.
- **Receive hold** — QEMU's e1000 model stops delivering received frames for one second after
  every write to RCTL (the receive control register). The manual describes no such behavior.
- **Isolated run**, **claim**, **qualified** — as in the [L02d3 evidence](L02d3.md#terms);
  **divergence** and its labels as in the [L02f1 evidence](L02f1.md#terms).

See the [glossary](../GLOSSARY.md), the [design](../QEMU-DIFFERENTIAL.md) (§5–§6, A4, A5,
A7), the [plan](../IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md) (L02f), and the [notebook chapter](../notebook/L02f2.md).

## Status

**Complete, 2026-09-25, after one repair round.** Round 1 repaired the E1 policy (L02f1's
V1). The repaired candidate binds, and past probe it passes 9 of 10 scenarios in the first
isolated rerun; the tenth, `down-during-traffic`, fails one check intermittently (3 of 6
isolated runs), for a cause outside the candidate: the reply the check waits for reaches the
guest's ICMP layer every time, and is lost above it while the model's receive hold delivers
it in one burst with about 30 frames left over from the stopped flood. The reference passes
6 of 6 because it reports carrier later, so its check runs after the hold. No candidate defect
was found past probe, so no second round was run; the budget left is two rounds. The harness
weakness is an open finding (H1).

## Round 1: the E1 policy

- **Run.** Codex ran for 89 s (16:46:39–16:48:08), exit 0, 109,812 input and 2,563 output
  tokens. The audit read 19,914 sandboxed-process log lines: workspace reads were the spec,
  the manual, the candidate and `kernel/include/linux/iopoll.h`, matching its `CONSULTED.md`;
  writes were the candidate and its three notes; no successful access outside the allowed
  roots; 13 failed attempts outside them (plugin manifests at `/`, `/.git`, `/sys` and
  `/var` probes by the CLI's runtime), each refused by the sandbox; endpoints as in the pilot.
  **Verdict PASS.** The first audit run reported four stdio pipes as outside accesses; that
  was a false positive in the audit script, fixed and tested in `fa86bc7` before the verdict
  above was taken.
- **Change.** One function, the E1 step in the candidate's EEPROM read: when EE_REQ or EE_GNT
  is set, clear EE_REQ and poll EECD every 5 µs for up to 10 ms for both to clear. If EE_REQ is
  still set, fail probe with `-EBUSY` as before. If only EE_GNT is set, log a warning and
  proceed with the EERD reads, which keep their own 10 ms timeout per word. The 10 ms bound is
  the implementer's choice, labeled as such. Source `dfb0aa0c…`.
- **Spec gap filed** by the implementer: "§5.3 E1 — How long should software wait after
  clearing EE_REQ for EE_GNT to clear, and should it attempt bounded EERD reads, use direct
  access, or fail when EE_REQ is clear but EE_GNT stays set?" This is L02f1's V1 secondary
  cause, now in the implementer's words; it goes to L02f3.
- **Build.** gcc-14 against the harness kernel: 0 warnings, and 0 warnings with `W=1`.
  Module `366782e4…`.
- **Operator review of the change.** The diff touches only E1 (one constant and one block);
  the poll terminates; the failure path is unchanged; the warning fires once per probe. On
  this model it adds the full 10 ms to each probe, since the grant never drops.

## Attribution: `down-during-traffic`

The check that fails is the DUT's three pings to the peer after the interface goes down under
a two-way flood and comes back up. In every failure, the reply to ping sequence 0 is the one
missing.

| Question | Answer | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does the reply reach the candidate? | Yes, in every run. The model's receive filter accepts it and the candidate consumes and refills that descriptor (RDT advances by one), in passing and failing runs alike | register trace, packet capture |
| Does it reach the guest's network stack? | Yes. In all six diagnostic runs, `Icmp: InEchoReps` rose by 3 across the three pings, including the five runs where ping reported 2 | `/proc/net/snmp` before and after, diagnostic runs `d1x*` |
| Why is the reply delayed? | Every RCTL write starts the model's one-second receive hold. Both drivers rewrite RCTL at carrier-up, when the stack joins multicast groups and the driver reprograms its filters. The candidate restarts autonegotiation on open and reports carrier from the link-change interrupt (0.52 s after `ip link set eth0 up`), so its hold runs to 1.52 s; the harness pings at 1.03 s. The reference reports carrier from its 2 s watchdog (2.06 s): hold to 3.06 s, ping at 3.02 s | QEMU 10.2.1 `hw/net/e1000.c`, `set_rx_control`, `e1000_can_receive` (operator-side reading); traces |
| What arrives with it? | The 30 to 36 echo requests the peer's flood sent after the DUT went down. They wait in the socket backlog and arrive in one burst when the hold ends: for the candidate at 1.52 s with the ARP reply and the seq-0 reply; for the reference at 1.0 s (47 frames), two seconds before its ping | traces, captures |
| Where is it lost? | Above ICMP: the reply is counted as received and not reported by ping. Every frame in the burst is ICMP, and a raw ICMP socket is handed a copy of each; busybox ping's socket losing the reply while about 30 larger frames are queued ahead of it fits the evidence, but the socket-level drop was not instrumented | counters above |
| Why only sometimes? | Whether ping drains its socket before the burst fills it is a race between the guest's receive processing and the ping process | 3 of 6 and 5 of 6 candidate runs |

The same hold is visible in every candidate run's first ping ("before the traffic"): sequence
0 takes about 490 ms and is not lost, because no leftover frames arrive with it.

**Divergence V6 (this unit's numbering continues L02f1's V1–V5).** Candidate intermittently
fails "DUT pings peer afterwards" in `down-during-traffic`; the reference passes. Label
`benign`, `[emulated]` result. Cause: model limitation (primary: the receive hold, which the
manual does not describe) and harness weakness (secondary, H1). Not an implementation error,
spec gap or spec error: the driver-visible sequence is the same in passing and failing runs,
and faster carrier detection is correct behavior.

**H1, open finding for the harness.** `down-during-traffic` checks the pings after the
interface returns, but (a) the peer's flood is stopped only after the DUT goes down, which
leaves frames for the DUT to receive later, and (b) the check runs as soon as carrier reads 1,
which on this model can fall inside the receive hold. A driver that reports carrier promptly
therefore fails this check at random. Fixing it (stop both floods before the interface goes
down, or wait out the hold before pinging) is a harness change, which would requalify Q15
(plan, L02f2). Not done here; see Open items.

## Decisions

- **No round 2.** The plan's round 2 takes failures found past probe; the one failure is not
  the candidate's (V6), so a brief to the implementer would carry no defect to fix. Stopping
  here follows the rule "stop early after a round that fixes nothing new" in spirit: nothing
  new needs fixing. Two rounds of the budget remain unspent.
- **No harness change in this unit.** A fix to H1 is a harness change and requalifies Q15;
  it is left to the user to place (Open items).
- **The diagnostic harness copy stays private.** It adds two `cat` commands and is used for
  attribution only; no verdict here is taken from it.

## Open items

- **H1** (harness): `down-during-traffic` pings inside the model's receive hold after a flood
  that leaves frames behind. Recommended: stop both floods before the interface goes down and
  wait at least a second after carrier before the pings, then requalify Q15 and rerun the
  candidate's `down-during-traffic`. Owner: the user's choice of unit (a harness unit before
  L02g, or an addition to L02f3).
- **For L02f3:** the implementer's E1 gap (above); L02f1's FWE observation; whether the spec
  should say anything about flow control (V10).
- The receive hold is a second model departure worth recording with the `[emulated]` class
  (L02g): after any RCTL write the model delivers nothing for one second.
