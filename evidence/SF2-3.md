# SF2-3: verification records and per-fact freshness

**Terms:**

- *verification record*: `resources/<name>.verify.yaml`, one verdict per fact of a spec file.
- *basis hash*: a fact's SHA-256 over its canonical form (`fact-v1`), the resource entries it
  cites, the assumptions it names and the bases of the facts it rests on (Merkle).
- *freshness status*: current, stale, upstream-stale, unverified or unknown, by comparing the
  verdict's recorded basis with the one computed now.
- *upstream map*: a verdict's `{<full reference>: <basis>}` for the facts in other roots its fact
  rests on; it is what lets upstream-stale be told apart from stale.
- *bookkeeping field*: a field of a cited entry that the basis leaves out (fetch dates, notes).
- *mutation check*: removes one guard to confirm a test notices.

All of these are in the [glossary](../GLOSSARY.md).

## What was built

- **`skills/spec-format/scripts/records.py`:** the canonical form `fact-v1` (sorted keys, no
  white space, UTF-8, NFC, integers only; floats, aliases and anything else the format does not
  hold are refused), basis hashes, reference cycles, the record checks and the freshness pass.
- **`spec.py check ... [--require-verified pr|main]`** (D19): stale, upstream-stale, unverified
  and unknown are errors under `pr`; under `main`, upstream-stale alone is a warning. Without the
  flag they are warnings.
- **`spec.py status <root>... [--stale] [--json]`**, with `check`'s root options: one row per
  fact (status, basis, recorded basis, changed upstream facts, second reader); exit 1 when the
  check found an error.
- **Record checks:** keys name facts, instance rows or variant rows (sub-keys refused until
  SF2-7); `summary` has five keys and matches the verdicts; `critical` facts need a second
  reader who is not the verdict's own verifier; carried verdicts are well formed; GAP belongs to
  gap facts only; records outside `resources/`, or named like a record but not one, are errors.
- **`verify.schema.json`:** the `upstream` map, and the carried-verdict rule (format 1 records
  carry no `contrary_evidence` or `citation_precision`).
- **Checker hooks** (`speccheck.py`): record discovery, and `Checker.reject`, which reports a
  rejected reference or citation and records it as failed in one step, so freshness reads every
  rejection as unknown.
- **Fixtures:** `records/verify_root` (board-expert's verify_root cases in format 2: current,
  stale, current FAIL, stale FAIL, malformed, none); the worked example's record.
- **Tests:** `test_records.py` (84 tests); SF2-2's exact-finding tests filter the new
  "unverified: no record" warning. The suite holds 251 tests.
- **Docs:** the design's Freshness section (upstream map, cycles, unknown, the bookkeeping list),
  `SKILL.md`, and the glossary's Upstream map row.

Consequential findings and their resolution:

- **Own root reached through another root** (round 1, both). `A#a → B#b → A#c`, edit `A#c`:
  B's recorded basis froze the path back into A, so `A#a` read upstream-stale. Rule: a fact is
  upstream-stale only when its whole dependency closure beyond the frontier stays outside its
  own root; otherwise stale. This also refuses a forged basis on an `A ↔ B` cycle.
- **Identity fields as an allow-list** (rounds 1 and 2). A document's `commit`, then a `files`
  item's `status` and a repos entry's `ref`, `status` and `role`, were left out, and the fact
  read current after a change. The redesign hashes every field of a cited entry except a closed
  bookkeeping list, and a test per kind changes every schema property once, so a field the
  schema gains fails that test until it is classified.
- **Rejections that freshness could not see** (rounds 1 and 2). Every rejected reference, and
  every rejected citation, is now recorded where it is reported (`Checker.reject`), and the
  citing fact reads unknown.

## Decisions

**User (2026-10-08):**

1. **Hash all but bookkeeping** (round 2, the stop rule): a cited entry counts whole except for
   its kind's named bookkeeping fields.

**Orchestrator, accepted before review:**

1. **The `upstream` map:** a verdict records the bases of the facts in other roots it rests on.
   Upstream-stale means recomputing with those recorded bases gives the verdict's basis and at
   least one of them moved; a current verdict's map must match exactly; with no map, an
   upstream change reads stale.
2. **Cycles:** a strongly connected component over `relates` is hashed as one, a digest over
   its members' local parts and their outside inputs; a cycle across two roots reads stale.
3. **`unknown`, a fifth status:** a basis that cannot be established is never current; a
   warning, an error under either mode.
4. **The implementer's interpretations** (notebook entry of 18:06):
   - a record's own defects and a current FAIL untrust a root; freshness findings are a policy
     on checked roots only;
   - a missing second reader is a warning, an error under both modes, reported only on a
     current verdict;
   - ADJUDICATE current is always a warning;
   - GAP is a gap fact's verdict both ways;
   - readers who disagree with a non-ADJUDICATE verdict are an error;
   - carried verdicts from format 1 omit `contrary_evidence` and `citation_precision`;
   - sub-keys are refused until SF2-7;
   - records outside `resources/`, or misnamed, are errors;
   - `status` takes `check`'s root options and exits 1 on an error.

**Orchestrator, review round 1 rulings:**

1. Upstream-stale requires the dependency closure beyond the frontier to stay outside the own
   root.
2. A document's `commit` counts.
3. A name listed twice makes every fact citing it unknown.
4. Every reference rejection makes the referring fact unknown, through one `Checker.reject`.
5. A `files` entry hashes by `path` and `license_from`; its `note` is bookkeeping (later
   widened by the user's round-2 decision to every field but `note`).
6. `status` text prints each error finding before its tally.
7. A reader with the verdict's own verifier (NFC, white space collapsed, case folded) is not a
   second reader.
8. In `resources/`, any name containing `verify` that is not a valid record is an error.
9. GAP on an instance or variant row is an error.
10. Plan acceptance 3 reads "a warning, an error under `--require-verified`".
11. Tests kill the reviewer's surviving mutants.

**Orchestrator, review round 2:** a cited repos entry pinned by a `ref` is unknown; a citation
the check rejects makes the fact unknown; GAP on a variant row gets its own test; the
own-verifier comparison's reach is recorded as a limitation.

**Orchestrator, review round 3:** the S1 tests land before the checkpoint; N1, N2 and round 2's
N2 are tracked without code change.

## The bookkeeping list

`records.BOOKKEEPING`, also in the design's Freshness section. Every other field counts, a
future one included.

| Kind | Bookkeeping (left out of the basis) |
| --- | --- |
| document | `verified`, `fetch`, `note` |
| repos entry | `verified`, `fetch`, `fetch_via`, `note` |
| a repos entry's `files` item | `note` |
| assumption | `todo` |

A repos entry contributes only the `files` items of the paths the fact cites, so listing
another path stales nothing. Layout (key order, comments, folding, flow or block style) and a
fact's `section` never count.

## Limitations (tracked)

Listed with impact in the plan's backlog. None lets a run pass that should fail, except the
first, which needs one verifier spelled two ways.

- **The own-verifier rule catches case only.** A fullwidth letter or a trailing period in a
  reader's verifier reads as a second reader. Verifiers are free text, and no normalization
  short of an identity registry closes this.
- **The first pass reads freshness without the rejected sets.** A current FAIL whose citation
  the check now rejects is reported as a current FAIL and also reads unknown: two errors where
  one would do.
- **One spec id in two files of a root** merges their facts' status rows into one. The check
  already reports the duplicate id as an error.
- **Out of scope here:** writing records (SF2-8's procedure), rendering status (SF2-4), the CI
  workflows using the modes (SF2-11), sub-keys (SF2-7).
