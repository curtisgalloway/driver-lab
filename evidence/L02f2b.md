# L02f2b: harness fix H1 and Q15 requalification

## Terms

- **Candidate** — `e1000_l02`, the driver written from the spec alone, as repaired in
  L02f2's round 1. **Reference** — Linux v6.12's `e1000`.
- **Receive hold** — QEMU's e1000 model delivers no received frame for one second after any
  write to RCTL (the receive control register); frames that arrive meanwhile are released
  in one burst when it ends. The manual describes no such behavior (L02f2, V6).
- **Leftover frames** — frames the peer's flood sent after the DUT's interface went down,
  delivered later in that burst.
- **Settling interval** — a documented, bounded wait after carrier returns and before the
  recovery pings.
- **H1** — L02f2's harness finding: `down-during-traffic` pinged as soon as carrier read 1,
  which could fall inside the receive hold, so a driver that reports carrier promptly failed
  at random.
- **Isolated run**, **claim**, **qualified**, **precondition** — as in the
  [L02d3 evidence](L02d3.md#terms); **d15** is L02d3's planted defect that keeps the receive
  ring indices across close and open.

See the [glossary](../GLOSSARY.md), the [plan](../IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md) (L02f2b), the
[L02f2 evidence](L02f2.md) (V6, H1) and the [notebook chapter](../notebook/L02f2b.md).

## Status

**Complete, 2026-09-25.** H1 is fixed and Q15 is requalified. On the fixed harness, in
isolated runs eight at a time, the reference passed `down-during-traffic` 10 of 10 and the
candidate 10 of 10, and d15 failed it 4 of 4 on the recovery pings, for the same reason as in
L02d3. The same load with the old harness reproduced H1 (candidate 4 of 8 failed). Q15 keeps
the scope "qualified, not traffic-specific", and the candidate's result for it is now PASS.

## The fix

Harness commit [`1f9f615`](https://github.com/curtisgalloway/driver-lab/commit/1f9f615)
(`l02harness.py` SHA-256 `57ef191b…`, the file every run below used; `guest-init.sh`
unchanged, `eccecebe…`). After review, a comment-only correction (the reasoning for 3 s,
R1 below) gave the final file `705694b9…`; `git diff` between the two changes comment
lines only.

- **Traffic through shutdown** stays as it was: both floods run until `ip link set eth0
  down` returns and are stopped only then, so the peer keeps sending into the outage.
- **Settling interval:** after carrier reads 1, the harness waits a fixed 3 s
  (`RECOVERY_SETTLE`, recorded as a host action), then pings once each way. No retry; the
  check names are unchanged.
- **New precondition,** checked from each run's register trace: "the pings afterwards
  began after the receive hold and the leftover burst" passes when the last RCTL write
  before the DUT's pings came at least 1 s earlier. Its detail records the time, the frames
  the model accepted between that write and the pings, and those accepted during the pings.
- Tests: the check (inside and outside the hold, reads and later writes ignored, no write)
  and the scenario's order with stand-in guests (floods stopped after the interface goes
  down, carrier, the 3 s wait, one ping each way).

**Why 3 s.** Both drivers rewrite RCTL at carrier-up, which starts a hold. In the 18 L02f2
traces (12 candidate, 6 reference; six of the candidate traces from L02f2's diagnostic
harness copy, whose RCTL timing matches the others) and in all 24 of this unit's, that
write came before the harness read carrier 1, so the hold ended at most 1 s after the read:
about 0.5 s after it for the candidate, and between 0 and about 1.0 s for the reference,
whose watchdog sometimes reports carrier just before a read (this unit's `ref1`, `ref6`,
`ref10`). 3 s is the 1 s hold plus at least 2 s of margin (observed: the pings started
2.005 s after the hold ended at the least), and would still cover a carrier-up RCTL write up
to about 1.9 s after the read.

## Limitations

- The settling interval is fixed at 3 s from 18 L02f2 traces and 24 of this unit's: one
  QEMU version, one host, KVM. A slower host or TCG may need more; the precondition fails
  loudly rather than letting a ping fall inside a hold unnoticed.
- The precondition sees only RCTL writes and the model's unicast-match events; it does not
  prove that no other model delay applies. It also ignores RCTL writes after the DUT's
  pings start: a driver that rewrote RCTL during the pings would start a new hold there and
  the failure would read as the driver's (none did in these runs; review R2).
- Its detail counts leftover frames from the last RCTL write onward. For the reference the
  burst comes earlier, at the end of the hold from open, so its detail shows 1 frame; the
  counts above are from the whole trace after interface-up.
- Qualification is defect-specific, as in L02d3: d15 shows the check catches a restart that
  leaves the receive ring empty.
- The other nine scenarios were not rerun here. The change touches only
  `down-during-traffic`, but the harness hash changed: L02f3's acceptance stage runs every
  scenario on this harness.
- Host load was matched by concurrency (eight at a time, as before), not by measuring it.
